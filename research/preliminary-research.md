## 3. Context: BC solar economics changed July 1, 2026 (design-critical)

- The BCUC approved BC Hydro's net metering overhaul (decision announced March 24, 2026;
  order G-64-26). The old **Net Metering Service Rate (RS 1289) is closed to new customers**;
  the new **Self-Generation Service Rate (RS 2289) applies from July 1, 2026**.
- **New mechanics:** surplus generation exported to the grid is bought at a **fixed 10¢/kWh**
  (net billing with instantaneous netting), credited each billing cycle instead of annually.
  The price is held for 5 years. Export limit: up to **100 kW per phase**.
- **Why this flips the design question:** retail electricity costs more than the export
  price (third-party summaries: roughly ~12–14¢/kWh residential steps), so **every kWh you
  self-consume is worth more than one you export**. Oversizing for pure export is now the
  expensive mistake; sizing against actual consumption, load-shifting, and storage are the
  valued moves. (A solar marketer's take: "the sunniest cities are the ones where oversizing
  now costs the most.")
- **Also new:** Community Generation Service Rate (RS 2290) — shared facilities up to 2 MW
  can sell surplus at 10¢/kWh (e.g. strata/multi-tenant arrangements), with injection limits
  set per benefitting customer (24 kW residential / 100 kW commercial).
- **Rebates (verify current terms at bchydro.com):** BC Hydro solar + battery rebates are
  advertised in the combined range of up to ~$5,000 (solar) and up to ~$1,500–$5,000 (battery,
  higher tier with Peak Saver participation); Solve Energy markets "up to $10,000" combined.
  **From June 1, 2026, solar and battery installs must be completed by a Home Performance
  Contractor Network (HPCN) member** to be rebate-eligible. PST exemption on solar equipment
  is also commonly cited.

## 4. Solar resource — Victoria / Vancouver Island

- **Typical production (third-party estimates):** ~**1,040 kWh per kW per year** (Xolar),
  on ~2,193 sunshine hours/yr — described as the strongest coastal solar resource in BC.
  Model-based report (solarcalculator.ca): ~1,548 kWh/m²/yr irradiance at an optimal
  tilt of **~37°** ⇒ ~1,330 kWh/kW·yr class output. Treat these as *indicative ranges*:
  model the actual site before quoting a number.
- **Seasonal shape (same model report, ~100 kWp example):** July ≈ 587 kWh/day avg vs
  December ≈ 130 kWh/day — i.e. winter makes roughly **one quarter** of summer output.
  The Nov–Feb deficit is the design pain point on the coast; storage and load-matching
  strategy is where teams can differentiate.
- **Authoritative tooling to use on the day:**
  - NREL **PVWatts** — https://pvwatts.nrel.gov (standard bankable estimator)
  - **NRCan photovoltaic potential maps** — official kWh/kWp and insolation data, ~2 km grid,
    municipality lookup — https://natural-resources.canada.ca/energy-sources/renewable-energy/photovoltaic-potential-solar-resource-maps-canada
  - **City of Victoria Solar Rooftop Calculator** — map-based, roof-specific production and
    savings — https://solarrooftop.victoria.ca/prod/public/index.php

## 5. Precedent: how these UVic design hackathons run

- **March 2026 (IESVic + BCSEA "Design Hackathon", Sat March 7):** 28 students in 6 teams,
  one day. Challenge: design a **standalone, off-grid renewable-powered e-bike charging
  station** for campuses/public spaces, using **real weather data and projected demand**.
  Teams covered solar, wind, battery storage, and cost analysis, and produced preliminary
  designs that were judged "thoughtful, technically credible."
- **Winning angles from that event:** modularity and siting flexibility; the problem framing
  that "grid connections are expensive, slow to permit, and not always available" (i.e.
  off-grid value proposition); credible capacity/cost analysis.
- **Takeaways for Sept 26:** expect a one-day "preliminary design" scope — concept + sizing +
  rough cost + a pitch — judged by industry people on technical credibility, realism, and
  communication. Same organizers' playbook, new (solar) challenge partner.
- **UREC track record** (for context): US DOE Solar Decathlon participation, UVic campus
  microgrid model, tidal power buoy design, UVic campus solar irradiance map, wind turbine
  design competition.

## 6. Fast-start playbook (draft)

**Likely deliverable set for a one-day design:** concept + block diagram
(array → controller/inverter → battery → loads/grid), sizing calcs, site/roof sketch,
component BOM with vendors, budget (±30%), savings/payback with rebate plan, risks &
assumptions, 5-minute pitch.

**Numbers to have memorized:**
- Export: 10¢/kWh fixed (RS 2289) — self-consumption beats export
- Retail: ~12–14¢/kWh residential steps (third-party figure; verify)
- Victoria yield: ~1,040 kWh/kW·yr (range to ~1,330 depending on model/tilt)
- Optimal fixed tilt ≈ 37°
- Rebates: up to ~$5k solar + up to ~$1.5–5k battery; HPCN install requirement
- Export limit: 100 kW/phase

**Sizing chain (typical):** annual load (kWh) → array DC kW = target generation ÷ site
specific yield → module count (typical modern module ~400–500 W, ~2 m² each) → inverter/MPPT
sizing (DC:AC ~1.2–1.35 typical) → battery kWh for backup critical loads and/or peak shifting
→ capex, rebates, payback.

**Differentiator ideas to keep in the back pocket:**
- "Self-consumption first" design methodology (the direct answer to the July 2026 rate change);
  storage + time-of-day shifting (on-peak 4–9 pm).
- Community/shared generation (new RS 2290) for multi-unit or campus buildings.
- Winter/coastal realities: cloud, snow, low winter sun; tilt and shading analysis.
- A rebate/incentive and permitting roadmap for the customer (Solve's brand is about guiding
  customers through this).

**Bring:** laptops, power strips, calculators; pre-downloaded PVWatts/SAM/pvlib + Victoria
TMY weather file; spreadsheet templates (energy balance, BOM, cost, payback); diagram tool
(draw.io); measuring tools if site assessment is involved.
